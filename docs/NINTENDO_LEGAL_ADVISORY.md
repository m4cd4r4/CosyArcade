# Nintendo Legal Advisory

CosyArcade must tread extremely carefully regarding Nintendo content. This document outlines Nintendo's litigious history and our policy for avoiding legal issues.

---

## Nintendo's Legal History

Nintendo is **the most aggressive** company in the gaming industry when it comes to protecting their intellectual property. They have a dedicated legal team and a zero-tolerance policy.

### Major Legal Actions

| Year | Target | Outcome |
|------|--------|---------|
| 2018 | LoveROMs / LoveRetro | $12 million settlement, sites shut down |
| 2021-22 | RomUniverse | $2.1 million judgment, permanent destruction order |
| 2024 | Yuzu Emulator | $2.4 million settlement, development ceased |
| 2024 | Ryujinx Emulator | Development ceased after Nintendo contact |

### Key Takeaways

1. **ROM Distribution**: Courts consistently rule against ROM distribution of copyrighted games
2. **Emulator Targeting**: Nintendo now targets emulators themselves (Yuzu case)
3. **Fan Projects**: Nintendo routinely issues DMCA takedowns for fan games
4. **No Exceptions**: Even non-commercial projects get targeted
5. **International Reach**: Nintendo pursues cases globally

---

## What We Must NEVER Do

### Absolutely Prohibited

- ❌ Host any commercial Nintendo ROMs (Mario, Zelda, Pokémon, etc.)
- ❌ Use Nintendo trademarks in marketing or SEO
- ❌ Display Nintendo box art or official screenshots
- ❌ Link to sites that host Nintendo ROMs
- ❌ Provide instructions for obtaining commercial Nintendo games
- ❌ Use Nintendo console names in ways that imply we host their games

### Grey Areas to Avoid

- ⚠️ Fan-made games using Nintendo characters (even if free)
- ⚠️ ROM hacks of Nintendo games
- ⚠️ Ports of Nintendo games (even if recreated from scratch)
- ⚠️ Games that are "inspired by" Nintendo franchises too closely
- ⚠️ Using Nintendo console colours/branding heavily

---

## What We CAN Safely Do

### Legally Safe

- ✅ Host original homebrew games for Nintendo hardware
- ✅ Use generic platform names ("NES-compatible", "8-bit console")
- ✅ Display developer-provided screenshots of homebrew
- ✅ Link to official homebrew developer sites
- ✅ Describe our platforms factually ("Nintendo Entertainment System released in 1983")

### Homebrew on Nintendo Platforms

Original homebrew games created by independent developers are **legal** to distribute if:

1. The game was created entirely by the developer (no Nintendo code)
2. The developer has given permission for distribution
3. The game doesn't use Nintendo characters/IP
4. We have documented proof of the legal source

---

## Platform Naming Strategy

Instead of promoting "NES Games" or "SNES Games", we should:

### Primary Description
> "Play original homebrew games created for classic 8-bit and 16-bit consoles"

### Platform Pages
- Use full historical names: "Nintendo Entertainment System" (factual)
- Focus on the homebrew community, not Nintendo
- Emphasise these are **new games by independent developers**

### Never Imply
- That we have Nintendo's commercial library
- That users can play Mario, Zelda, etc.
- That we're a "ROM site"

## DMCA Response Plan

If Nintendo (or anyone) sends a takedown notice:

1. **Immediate Response** (within 24 hours)
   - Remove the content in question
   - Do NOT dispute Nintendo takedowns
   - Document the removal

2. **Review**
   - How did this content get through?
   - Update curation process

3. **Communication**
   - Respond professionally to the notice
   - Confirm removal
   - Do NOT argue or negotiate

## Competitor Analysis

Sites that have survived:
- **Archive.org** - Non-profit status, library exception arguments
- **RetroAchievements** - Doesn't host ROMs, just achievement data
- **NESDev** - Community/educational focus, links but doesn't host

Sites that were shut down:
- EmuParadise (preemptive shutdown)
- CoolROM (restricted Nintendo content)
- Most ROM sites that hosted Nintendo content

## Our Position Statement

**CosyArcade is NOT a ROM site.**

We are a curated platform for **legally-free homebrew games**. We do not host, link to, or facilitate access to copyrighted commercial games. Every game in our library was created by independent developers who have granted distribution rights.
